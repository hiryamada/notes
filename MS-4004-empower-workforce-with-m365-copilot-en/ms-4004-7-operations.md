
# Microsoft Copilot Demo for Operations: Exercise 1

## Demo Overview

In this scenario, an operations professional reviews an everyday business process and considers improvements.

Use Microsoft Copilot to organize current activities, analyze challenges, consider improvements, and prepare explanations for stakeholders.

1. Organize the current business process
2. Compare the current and improved processes
3. Create a business process improvement proposal
4. Summarize the proposal for stakeholders

> **Demo flow: Visualize → Compare → Improve → Communicate**

The total demo duration is 50 minutes.

# Exercise 1-1: Visualize the Business Process

## App

[Microsoft Whiteboard](https://whiteboard.cloud.microsoft/)

## Situation

You are reviewing the internal inquiry-handling process.

First, organize a general business process from receiving an inquiry to responding and closing it.

Use a general inquiry-handling example without relying on a specific internal system or actual operational data.

## Prompt

```text
Organize a general current-state process for handling internal inquiries.

Include a flow such as:

1. Receive the inquiry
2. Review the inquiry details
3. Assign a responsible person
4. Research the necessary information
5. Draft a response
6. Respond to the requester
7. Provide additional support as needed
8. Close the inquiry

For each step, add representative tasks performed by the responsible person.

Keep it simple and easy to understand as a starting point for discussing process improvements.
```

## Expected Outcome

The inquiry-handling process is visually organized into multiple steps.

You can review points such as:

- Where human judgment is needed
- Where information searches are needed
- Where waiting time is likely to occur
- Where repetitive work exists
- Which areas may be improved

> In Whiteboard, focus on creating a starting point that people can review and discuss, rather than a finished workflow.

# Exercise 1-2: Compare Current and Improved Operations

## App

[Open Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat](https://copilot.cloud.microsoft/home)

## Situation

A review of the current inquiry-handling process suggests that information searches, response drafting, and handoffs may be time-consuming.

Next, consult Copilot to consider how the process might change if manual work were reduced.

## Prompt

```text
We want to improve our internal inquiry-handling process.

Currently, after receiving an inquiry, the responsible person reviews its details, searches for the necessary information, drafts a response, and replies to the requester.

For potential improvements to this process, organize a comparison table covering:

1. Current operations
2. Potential improvement areas
3. Improved operations
4. Expected benefits
5. Points where human review or judgment should remain

Rather than simply automating everything, distinguish tasks that require human judgment from those that can be made more efficient.
```

## Expected Outcome

Current and improved operations are compared.

Potential improvements include:

- Organizing inquiry details
- Searching for relevant information
- Drafting responses
- Comparing with past inquiries
- Summarizing actions taken
- Creating reusable knowledge

At the same time, tasks requiring human judgment are identified, such as:

- Final review of responses
- Handling exceptional inquiries
- Determining priorities
- Making organizational decisions

## Follow-Up Prompt

```text
Select three improvements that are relatively easy to introduce and may also reduce working time.

For each, organize the following.

- Current challenge
- Improvement method
- Expected benefits
- Implementation considerations
```

# Exercise 1-3: Document a Process Improvement Proposal

## App

[Open Word](https://word.cloud.microsoft/)

## Situation

Potential improvements to the inquiry-handling process have been organized.

Next, create a process improvement proposal to discuss with stakeholders.

Start with a new Word document without using existing internal documents.

## Prompt

```text
Create a proposal to improve the internal inquiry-handling process.

Currently, after receiving an inquiry, the responsible person reviews its details, searches for the necessary information, and drafts a response.

Assume that information searches, response drafting, and organizing records of actions taken are particularly time-consuming.

Use the following structure.

1. Background
2. Current business process
3. Currently anticipated challenges
4. Direction for improvement
5. Improved business process
6. Expected benefits
7. Items to confirm before implementation
8. Next steps

Do not make definitive statements about organization-specific information. Mark items that require verification of actual operations as "To be confirmed."

Keep the content concise and practical so both operations staff and managers can understand it.
```

## Expected Outcome

Word creates a document for discussing process improvements with stakeholders.

It organizes topics such as:

- Current operations
- Potential bottlenecks
- Improvement candidates
- Points where human judgment should remain
- Expected benefits
- Implementation considerations
- Next actions

## Follow-Up Prompt: Clarify the Before and After

```text
Add a Before / After comparison table that clearly shows the differences between current and improved operations.
```

## Follow-Up Prompt: Shorten for Managers

```text
Add an executive summary at the beginning of this document that a manager can read in about one minute.
```

# Exercise 1-4: Create Stakeholder Presentation Materials

## App

[Open PowerPoint](https://powerpoint.cloud.microsoft/)

## Situation

The direction for process improvement has been established.

Next, create a short presentation for a meeting with managers and related departments.

## Prompt

```text
Create a stakeholder presentation about improving the internal inquiry-handling process.

Include the following.

1. The current inquiry-handling process
2. Currently anticipated main challenges
3. Direction for improvement
4. Differences between current and improved operations
5. Expected benefits
6. Points where human review or judgment should continue
7. Next steps

Keep the presentation concise enough to deliver in no more than 10 minutes.

Prioritize helping stakeholders understand how operations will change rather than providing detailed technical explanations.
```

## Expected Outcome

PowerPoint creates presentation materials with a structure such as:

1. Background to the process improvement
2. Current inquiry handling
3. Current challenges
4. Proposed improvements
5. Before / After
6. Expected benefits
7. Areas requiring human judgment
8. Next steps

## Follow-Up Prompt

```text
Restructure the presentation around Before / After comparisons so the differences between current and improved operations are clear at a glance.
```

# Exercise 1 Summary

In this demo, an operations professional used Microsoft Copilot to improve everyday work as follows.

| Stage | Activity | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Visualize** | Visualize the current business process | Microsoft Whiteboard |
| **Compare** | Compare current and improved operations | [Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat](https://copilot.cloud.microsoft/home) |
| **Improve** | Document a process improvement proposal | [Word](https://word.cloud.microsoft/) |
| **Communicate** | Explain the proposed improvements to stakeholders | [PowerPoint](https://powerpoint.cloud.microsoft/) |

> The key point of this exercise is not to delegate process improvement decisions to Copilot, but to organize current operations, explore improvement opportunities, and prepare material for stakeholder discussion.

---

# Demo Delivery Tips

This exercise is designed to minimize the need to prepare demo-specific data in advance.

- Existing Teams chats: Not used
- Existing Outlook emails: Not used
- Existing operational documents: Not used
- Actual inquiry history: Not used
- Actual customer data: Not used

This allows the same demo story to be reproduced without depending on participants' environments or real data.

For actual process improvements, people must review organization-specific processes, rules, systems, and data, then validate Copilot's suggestions.
